# How to Buy Neuralink Stock Before the IPO

Neuralink, the brain-computer interface company founded in 2016 by Elon Musk and a team of neuroscientists and engineers, is one of the most searched-for private companies on the market — and one of the hardest to invest in. There is no ticker, no public float, and no announced IPO date.

This guide sets out what Neuralink has actually raised, the realistic routes to exposure, and where tokenized pre-IPO access fits.

## What Neuralink has raised, and when

Neuralink's funding history is a matter of public record, but the valuations attached to it are mostly press-reported rather than company-disclosed. The verified milestones:

- **August 2023 — Series D, $280 million.** Led by Founders Fund, with a post-money valuation reported at roughly $5 billion. A further $43 million extension followed in November 2023.
- **June 2025 — Series E, $650 million.** Announced by the company on 2 June 2025. Participants included ARK Invest, DFJ Growth, Founders Fund, G42, Lightspeed, QIA, Sequoia Capital, Thrive Capital, Valor Equity Partners, and Vy Capital. The pre-money valuation was reported at about $9 billion; Neuralink itself did not disclose a figure.

Alongside the capital raises, the company received FDA clearance for its first-in-human trial in May 2023, implanted its first patient in January 2024, and has since been granted FDA Breakthrough Device designations for its vision and speech programmes.

Reports since then of a further round and of secondary-market marks well above the Series E price vary widely between sources and have not been confirmed by the company. Treat any headline valuation you see quoted as indicative, not as a price you can transact at.

## Why Neuralink is hard to buy

Like every hot private company, Neuralink sells equity through negotiated rounds to a short list of venture, growth, and sovereign investors. The shareholder base is deliberately narrow, transfers require company consent, and there is no order book to hit. Retail investors have historically had no direct route in.

Two factors make Neuralink unusual even among private companies. It is a **clinical-stage medical device business**, so its value depends on regulatory milestones and trial outcomes as much as on commercial traction. And it remains majority-controlled by its founder, which shapes both governance and the timing of any liquidity event.

## The routes available today

- **Employee equity.** The most direct path — and only if you work there. Options vest over years and are subject to transfer restrictions.
- **Pre-IPO and thematic funds.** Some funds hold Neuralink in a diversified basket alongside other private names. You buy the fund, not the shares, typically as an accredited investor with a high minimum.
- **Secondary marketplaces.** Brokered transfers of existing shares. Access is gated to accredited investors, minimums run high, and the company can block the transfer.

## Where tokenized pre-IPO access fits

Tokenization represents pre-IPO economic exposure as an on-chain token that tracks a private-company outcome, rather than granting registered shares. The aim is broader access: smaller minimums, transparent on-chain pricing, and faster settlement than a brokered secondary.

A token does not put you on Neuralink's cap table, and it carries its own risks — read the [compliance reference](/compliance) before assuming any particular regulatory treatment. Neuralink is currently listed as a coming-soon asset on the [transparency page](/transparency).

## What to check before you invest

1. **What exactly do you own?** A share, a fund unit, or a token tracking an outcome — the three behave very differently at IPO.
2. **Regulatory risk.** Neuralink's value is tied to FDA decisions and clinical results, which can move it sharply in either direction.
3. **Liquidity.** Can you exit before a listing, and at what cost?
4. **Eligibility.** Accreditation and jurisdiction rules still apply to most routes.
5. **Fees and lockups.** Pre-IPO products often carry carry, spreads, or transfer restrictions.

Pre-IPO investing is high risk and illiquid. Nothing here is investment advice — do your own research and consult a licensed adviser.
