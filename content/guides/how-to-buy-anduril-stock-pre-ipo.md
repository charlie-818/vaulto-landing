# How to Buy Anduril Stock Before the IPO

Anduril Industries is the most valuable venture-backed defense company in the world, and it is still private. Its founders have said an IPO is coming "definitely" — but not yet. Until then, anyone who wants exposure to Anduril stock has to work within the same tight constraints that apply to every hot private company.

This guide covers what Anduril does, what it was last valued at, the realistic routes to exposure, and where Vaulto's implied-valuation markets fit.

## What Anduril does

Anduril was founded in 2017 by Palmer Luckey (the creator of the Oculus headset) alongside Brian Schimpf, Trae Stephens, Matt Grimm and Joe Chen. Its pitch is to run a defense contractor like a software company: fund its own R&D, build products first, and sell them to militaries as finished systems rather than winning cost-plus development contracts.

The core of the business is **Lattice**, an AI-driven command-and-control platform that fuses data from sensors, drones and other hardware into a single operating picture and lets a small number of operators direct many autonomous systems at once. Around Lattice, Anduril builds and sells hardware including:

- **Sentry towers and counter-drone systems** for border and base security.
- **Ghost and Bolt drones** and the Roadrunner reusable interceptor.
- **Fury**, an autonomous fighter jet developed for the U.S. Air Force's Collaborative Combat Aircraft programme.
- **Undersea vehicles, solid rocket motors and munitions**, increasingly built at scale in its own factories.

In 2025 the company took over the U.S. Army's soldier headset programme from Microsoft and began building Arsenal-1, a large manufacturing plant in Ohio. Revenue roughly doubled in 2025 to about $2.2 billion, according to CEO Brian Schimpf.

Valuations in private rounds are set by negotiation between the company and a small group of investors. They are a useful reference point, not a market price — which is exactly the gap that implied-valuation markets try to fill.

## Why retail investors can't buy Anduril directly

Anduril has no ticker and no public order book. Its shares are held by employees, its founders, and a short list of venture and growth investors who buy into each funding round. Private companies are under no obligation to sell to the public, and Anduril's rounds have been heavily oversubscribed — the Series G was reportedly more than eight times oversubscribed — so there is no shortage of institutional demand to absorb every share on offer.

The routes that do exist are narrow:

- **Primary rounds.** Reserved for large funds and strategic partners. Not accessible to the public.
- **Employee equity.** Only if you work there, and subject to vesting and transfer restrictions.
- **Pre-IPO and defense-tech funds.** A handful of funds hold Anduril inside a diversified portfolio. You buy the fund, not the shares, usually as an accredited investor with a high minimum and the fund's fees on top.
- **Secondary transfers.** Existing holders occasionally sell through brokered deals. These are gated to accredited investors, priced opaquely, and the company can block the transfer.

For most people, none of these are available at any price.

Vaulto takes a different approach. Instead of trying to acquire Anduril shares, it lets you trade exposure to Anduril's **implied valuation** — the value the market collectively expects the company to be worth when it goes public.

That implied valuation is derived from prediction markets on Polymarket, which run events on which valuation tier a company will IPO at. Vaulto reads the live odds across those tiers and converts them into a single implied valuation, so the number you see updates continuously as traders reprice the outcome.

## Risks specific to Anduril

- **Contract concentration.** Anduril's revenue depends heavily on U.S. and allied government budgets. Programme cancellations, procurement delays or a change in defense priorities can move the business sharply.
- **Manufacturing execution.** The company is spending aggressively on factories. Scaling production of missiles, drones and rocket motors is capital-intensive and hard to do on schedule.
- **Valuation step-ups.** Doubling from $30.5B to $61B in under a year sets a high bar. A slower-growth round, or a down round, would reset expectations.
- **IPO timing.** Management has signalled an IPO is planned but has not committed to a date. Prediction markets can stay open for a long time, and long-dated positions carry more uncertainty.
- **Geopolitics.** Demand for defense technology is tied to conflicts and political cycles that are impossible to forecast.

Pre-IPO exposure of any kind is high risk and illiquid. Implied-valuation positions add prediction-market risk on top: thin liquidity on some tiers, resolution rules that you must read carefully, and prices that can gap on news.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy Anduril stock right now?**
Not as a retail investor. Anduril is private, and its shares are only available to institutional investors in funding rounds, to employees, and occasionally to accredited investors via brokered secondaries.

**What is Anduril's current valuation?**
Its last confirmed post-money valuation was $61 billion, set in the $5 billion Series H announced on 13 May 2026. A round at around $100 billion was reported to be under discussion in July 2026 but had not been confirmed as closed when this guide was written.

**When is the Anduril IPO?**
No date has been announced. Founder Palmer Luckey has said the company will "definitely" go public, and chairman Trae Stephens has described preparing for a listing in the medium term.

**Does a Vaulto position give me Anduril shares?**
No. A Vaulto position tracks Anduril's implied IPO valuation through prediction markets. It is a directional bet on an outcome, not equity, and it can lose its full value.

**What's the ticker symbol for Anduril?**
There isn't one. Anduril has not filed for an IPO and has no public listing on any exchange.
