# How to Buy Stripe Stock Before the IPO

Stripe is one of the most valuable private technology companies in the world, and its founders have been in no hurry to take it public. That leaves a familiar question for anyone watching the payments giant: **how do you get exposure to Stripe stock while it is still private?**

This guide walks through the realistic routes, who qualifies for each, and where tokenized pre-IPO access fits in.

## Why Stripe is hard to buy

Private companies are not obligated to sell shares to the public. Stripe's equity sits with its founders, employees, and a concentrated group of venture and institutional backers who participate in negotiated funding rounds and tender offers. There is no ticker to look up and no order book to hit.

Historically, three groups could get in: employees with stock options, venture and growth funds, and accredited investors invited into secondary transactions. Everyone else was locked out — even as the company periodically ran tender offers that let insiders sell at a set valuation.

## The traditional routes

- **Employee equity.** The most direct path — and only if you work there. Options and RSUs vest over years and are subject to company transfer restrictions, with liquidity limited to the tenders Stripe chooses to run.
- **Venture and pre-IPO funds.** Some funds hold Stripe in a portfolio. You buy the fund, not the shares, and typically need to be accredited with a sizeable minimum.
- **Secondary marketplaces.** Platforms that match existing shareholders with buyers. Access is usually gated to accredited investors, minimums run high, spreads are wide, and Stripe can block transfers it does not approve.

## Where tokenized pre-IPO access fits

Tokenization aims to lower the barriers above by representing pre-IPO economic exposure as an on-chain token. Instead of negotiating a private secondary transfer or waiting for the next tender window, you hold a token whose value tracks a pre-IPO outcome. This can mean smaller minimums, faster settlement, and transparent on-chain pricing drawn from Stripe's implied valuation.

It does not turn you into a registered Stripe shareholder, and it carries its own risks — read the [compliance reference](/compliance) before assuming any particular regulatory treatment.

## What to check before you invest

1. **What exactly do you own?** A share, a fund unit, or a token tracking an outcome — the three behave very differently at IPO.
2. **Liquidity.** Can you exit before a listing, and at what cost?
3. **Eligibility.** Accreditation and jurisdiction rules still apply to most routes.
4. **Fees and lockups.** Pre-IPO products often carry carry, spreads, or transfer restrictions.

Pre-IPO investing is high risk and illiquid. Nothing here is investment advice — do your own research and consult a licensed adviser.
