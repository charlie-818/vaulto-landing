# How to Invest in Pre-IPO AI Companies

The most valuable AI companies — OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and others — are still private, and many are in no hurry to list. That creates a familiar problem: the businesses generating the most excitement are the hardest to invest in.

This sector guide explains the routes into pre-IPO AI, the risks specific to the space, and where tokenized access fits.

## Why pre-IPO AI is different

AI has attracted unusually large private rounds at valuations that can move sharply between raises. That means two things for investors: entry points are scarce, and the mark-to-market swings can be violent. Concentration risk is real — a handful of names dominate the narrative.

## The routes available today

- **Direct rounds.** Effectively closed to the public; reserved for strategic and institutional capital.
- **Thematic pre-IPO funds.** Diversified baskets of private AI companies. Convenient, but you inherit the fund's fees and its picks, and most require accreditation.
- **Secondary marketplaces.** Brokered transfers of existing shares, gated to accredited investors with high minimums and company-controlled transfers.

## Where tokenized pre-IPO access fits

Tokenization represents pre-IPO economic exposure as an on-chain token that tracks a private-company outcome. For a fast-moving sector like AI, the appeal is transparent pricing, smaller minimums, and the ability to build a diversified basket without a broker in the middle.

Tokens track outcomes rather than granting registered shares. ## What to check before you invest

1. **Diversification.** One or two names can dominate a "sector" position.
2. **The instrument.** Fund unit versus outcome token changes your risk and payoff.
3. **Liquidity.** Private exposure can be impossible to exit before a listing.
4. **Eligibility and fees.** Accreditation, jurisdiction, carry, and lockups all vary by route.

Pre-IPO investing is high risk and illiquid. Nothing here is investment advice — do your own research and consult a licensed adviser.
