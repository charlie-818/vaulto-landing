# How to Buy Databricks Stock Before the IPO

Databricks builds the data and AI platform that a large share of enterprise machine learning runs on, and it has stayed private far longer than most companies of its scale. That leaves would-be investors with a single question: **how do you get exposure to Databricks stock while it is still private?**

This guide walks through the realistic routes, who qualifies for each, and where tokenized pre-IPO access fits in.

## Why Databricks is hard to buy

Databricks grew out of the team behind Apache Spark at UC Berkeley and sells what it calls a lakehouse — a single platform for storing data, querying it, and training and serving models on top of it. Its open-source projects, including Delta Lake, MLflow, and Unity Catalog, are widely deployed, which is part of why the company is a familiar name to people who cannot buy a share of it.

It is private by choice, not by obligation. Databricks has funded itself through repeated late-stage private rounds rather than a listing, and its leadership has publicly said it is in no hurry to go public. Equity sits with employees, founders, and the venture and growth investors who joined those rounds. There is no ticker to look up and no order book to hit.

## The traditional routes

- **Employee equity.** The most direct path — and only if you work there. Options and RSUs vest over years and are subject to company transfer restrictions.
- **Venture and pre-IPO funds.** Some growth and crossover funds hold Databricks in a portfolio. You buy the fund, not the shares, and typically need to be accredited with a sizeable minimum and a multi-year lockup.
- **Secondary marketplaces.** Platforms that match existing shareholders with buyers. Access is usually gated to accredited investors, minimums run high, spreads to the last round price can be wide, and the company can block transfers.
- **Company-run tender offers.** Databricks has, as widely reported, let employees sell into structured liquidity events alongside funding rounds. These are for existing holders — outside investors cannot buy in unless invited.

## Where tokenized pre-IPO access fits

Tokenization aims to lower the barriers above by representing pre-IPO economic exposure as an on-chain token. Instead of negotiating a private secondary transfer and waiting on transfer-agent paperwork, you hold a token whose value tracks a pre-IPO outcome. This can mean smaller minimums, faster settlement, and transparent on-chain pricing.

It does not turn you into a registered Databricks shareholder, it carries no voting or information rights, and it carries its own risks — read the [compliance reference](/compliance) before assuming any particular regulatory treatment.

## What to check before you invest

1. **What exactly do you own?** A share, a fund unit, or a token tracking an outcome — the three behave very differently at IPO.
2. **Liquidity.** Can you exit before a listing, and at what cost?
3. **Eligibility.** Accreditation and jurisdiction rules still apply to most routes.
4. **Fees and lockups.** Pre-IPO products often carry carry, spreads, or transfer restrictions.
5. **Price reference.** Private marks come from the last round or a negotiated secondary print, not a continuous market — treat any headline number as stale until you see its source and date.

Pre-IPO investing is high risk and illiquid. Nothing here is investment advice — do your own research and consult a licensed adviser.
