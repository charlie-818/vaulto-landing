# How to Buy OpenAI Stock Before the IPO

OpenAI sits at the center of the AI boom, and demand to invest in it has far outstripped the ways to actually do so. There is no public stock, and the company's structure makes ordinary equity ownership more complicated than most startups.

This guide explains what you can realistically buy today and how tokenized pre-IPO exposure works.

## OpenAI's unusual structure

OpenAI is not a conventional venture-backed company. Its commercial arm sits under a capped-profit structure governed by a nonprofit parent. Investors hold profit-participation interests rather than ordinary common shares, which shapes both the upside and how any future liquidity event might unfold.

The practical takeaway: "buying OpenAI stock" rarely means buying plain equity. Understand the instrument before you commit capital.

## The routes available today

- **Direct rounds.** Reserved for large strategic and institutional investors. Not open to the public.
- **Pre-IPO and AI-focused funds.** Some funds hold OpenAI exposure. You buy into the fund, usually as an accredited investor with a high minimum.
- **Secondary transfers.** Existing holders occasionally sell interests through brokered secondary deals. Access is gated and the company controls transfers.

## Where tokenized pre-IPO access fits

Tokenization represents pre-IPO economic exposure as an on-chain token, tracking a private-company outcome rather than granting registered shares. The goal is broader access: lower minimums, transparent pricing, and faster settlement than a brokered secondary.

Because OpenAI's own equity is unusual, pay close attention to what a given token actually references. See the [compliance reference](/compliance) for how these instruments are classified.

## What to check before you invest

1. **The instrument.** Profit interest, fund unit, or outcome token — each behaves differently.
2. **Liquidity and exit.** Private positions can be impossible to sell before a listing.
3. **Eligibility.** Accreditation and jurisdiction requirements still apply to most routes.
4. **Fees and lockups.** Watch for carry, spreads, and transfer restrictions.

AI valuations move fast in both directions. Pre-IPO investing is high risk and illiquid — this is not investment advice.
